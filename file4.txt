a) significance level 𝛼: how likely is our test findings to yield a wrong conclusion.
𝛼 commonly 1%, 5% or 10%
the probability of incorrectly rejecting the null hypothesis when it is, in fact, true.
b) calc test statistics 
test statistic quantifies how many standard deviations the sample mean is from the hypothesized population mean
see "DSML\week4\D3\stepByStepHypTesting.png"
When 𝜎 is known, the appropriate test statistic is the z-score. Otherwise: or #samples <30 --> t-statistic is appropriate
c) p value 
If the p-value is low, it means that the likelihood of obtaining the observed results, or more  extreme  results,  under H0	is unlikely. This often leads to rejecting the null hypothesis in favor of an alternative hypothesis. The p-value tells us how likely our results are if the null hypothesis were true
d) decision
eather reject H_0 "p value < 𝛼" OR _fail_ to reject H_0 when "p value > 𝛼"
